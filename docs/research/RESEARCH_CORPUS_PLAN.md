# InkTelligence Research Corpus Plan

## Purpose

This plan defines the 30 source papers that will be accepted as the sole factual basis for the next compendium, technique, and history update:

- 10 papers covering all 66 catalogued tattoo styles;
- 10 papers covering all 44 catalogued techniques;
- 10 papers expanding all historical periods and anthropological lenses.

Alexei's persona shapes the questions: human meaning before aesthetics, technique grounded in skin and anatomy, long-term legibility, cultural integrity, and honest treatment of uncertainty. The persona is an editorial lens, never a factual source.

## Source-of-truth rule for the next update

1. Do not update SQL or the JSON fallback from this plan alone.
2. The uploaded papers and their bibliographies will be the only factual evidence admitted to the next update.
3. Every factual claim imported into the database must point to a paper ID, page or section, and evidence status.
4. Unsupported catalog claims must be removed, marked as disputed, or retained only as an explicitly labelled hypothesis.
5. Final database language remains English, even when a source paper is uploaded in another language.
6. Indigenous or living cultural traditions require community-authored or community-approved sources wherever available.

## Required structure of every uploaded paper

Each paper should contain:

1. Abstract and scope.
2. Definitions and boundary criteria.
3. Chronology, geography, and transmission.
4. Per-entry description for every assigned catalog ID.
5. Materials, tools, and making process where relevant.
6. Visual grammar: line, value, color, composition, motifs, and scale.
7. Skin, anatomy, placement, healing, aging, and technical limitations.
8. Social meaning, identity, ritual, memory, or client narrative.
9. Cultural ownership, sacred/restricted elements, and ethical use.
10. Disputes, regional variation, source limitations, and confidence level.
11. A database-ready appendix with one row per assigned ID and page-level citations.
12. Full bibliography with DOI, ISBN, archive, museum, or institutional identifiers when available.

## A. Ten style papers

### STY-01 — From Sailor Flash to Neo-Traditional: The Genealogy of Western Traditional Tattooing

Trace maritime, military, circus, commercial flash, revival, and illustrative expansion. Distinguish Old School, American Traditional, Neo-Traditional, New School, and historically informed flash revival by chronology and visual grammar.

### STY-02 — Skin as Image: Realism, Portraiture, Miniaturization, Hyperrealism, and Surreal Transformation

Study how photography, painting, portraiture, scale, color, and illusion moved into tattooing. Include the technical and aging limits of micro detail and the difference between accurate representation and surreal recombination.

### STY-03 — Black as Structure: Blackwork, Dotwork, Geometry, Ornament, Blackout, and Black-and-Grey

Define the boundary between style, compositional system, and technique. Examine solid fill, dot density, mathematical repetition, sacred-geometric claims, ornamental systems, negative skin, and long-term contrast.

### STY-04 — Living Lineages: Indigenous and Heritage Tattoo Systems, Colonial Disruption, Revival, and Cultural Authority

Cover Māori, Sāmoan, broader Polynesian, Marquesan, Haida/Pacific Northwest, Celtic, and Mesoamerican references without collapsing them into a generic “tribal” category. Modern Tribal must be analyzed separately as a Western adaptation. Community authority and restricted marks are central.

### STY-05 — The Japanese Tattoo Continuum: Irezumi, Tebori, Neo-Japanese, and Anime/Manga Translation

Connect Edo visual culture, body-suit composition, iconographic relationships, hand methods, machine practice, legal stigma, revival, and contemporary popular-media adaptation. Distinguish historical lineage from surface imitation.

### STY-06 — Paper, Canvas, and Screen Translated to Skin: Illustrative Tattoo Languages

Compare etching/woodcut, illustration, sketch, watercolor, abstraction, Pop Art/comic, and cartoon tattooing. Explain how marks native to other media are technically reconstructed in skin and how those translations age.

### STY-07 — The Experimental Body: Late-Modern, Post-Digital, Hybrid, and Anti-Canonical Tattoo Styles

Cover Trash Polka, biomechanical, bio-organic, sticker/patchwork, embroidery/patch, Cyber Sigilism, Neo-Tribal, Ignorant, glitch/pixel, and 3D/optical illusion. Document authorship, emergence, platform influence, hybridity, and contested naming.

### STY-08 — Economy of the Line and the Urban Letter: Fine-Line, Minimalism, Lettering, Chicano, and Graffiti

Study reduction, continuous line, single-needle aesthetics, script construction, calligraphy/calligraffiti, Chicano visual culture, and graffiti translation. Address cultural origin, prison and street histories, typography, scale, and longevity.

### STY-09 — Darkness, Devotion, and the Sublime: Horror, Occult, Gothic, and Religious Tattooing

Analyze fear, mortality, sacred imagery, devotion, transgression, memorial use, and dark aesthetics. Separate iconographic subject from execution style and document restrictions around living religious symbols.

### STY-10 — Functional and Material Exceptions: UV, White Ink, Negative Space, Silhouette, Handpoke, and Paramedical Tattooing

Cover styles defined by pigment behavior, absence of ink, simplified figure/ground, application method, or therapeutic/cosmetic purpose. Include evidence-based safety, fading, visibility across skin tones, and the boundary between art and clinical practice.

## B. Ten technique papers

### TEC-01 — The Constructed Line: Needle Grouping, Motion, Pressure, and Seven Linework Systems

Define fine, bold, broken, continuous, single-needle, script, and tapered line construction. Compare tool configuration, hand motion, placement, failure modes, and healed line behavior.

### TEC-02 — Building Value in Skin: Seven Shading Systems from Soft Gradients to Crosshatching

Compare soft, hard, whip, pepper, black-and-grey wash, crosshatching, and gradient blending through motion, dilution, density, trauma, visual effect, and healing.

### TEC-03 — Chromatic Practice: Packing, Shading, Blending, Highlights, White Ink, and Watercolor Effects

Explain pigment placement, saturation, transitions, white behavior, color interaction with skin tone, watercolor simulation, fading, and safety limits without presenting unverified pigment claims as settled fact.

### TEC-04 — Point by Point: Dotwork, Stippling, and Dot-Gradient Construction

Separate the three related systems by intent, spacing, density, tool configuration, scale, and healed appearance. Include geometric and tonal applications.

### TEC-05 — Constructing Illusion: Realistic Rendering, Depth Effects, and Optical Tattoo Techniques

Study reference translation, value mapping, perspective, highlights, cast shadow, trompe-l'œil, anatomical deformation, and the difference between fresh-image impact and durable readability.

### TEC-06 — Before and Beyond the Machine: Hand Poke, Tebori, Hand-Tapped Tatau, and Skin Stitching

Compare pre-electric and living manual technologies without treating them as interchangeable. Tools, gestures, apprenticeship, ritual authority, pain, healing, and community ownership must be documented separately.

### TEC-07 — Black, Skin, and Precision: Large Fill, Negative Space, Geometry, Silhouette, and Tribal Blackfill

Study saturation, edge control, skin as active shape, repeat geometry on curved anatomy, large-area trauma, retouch expectations, and cultural restrictions on traditional pattern systems.

### TEC-08 — Simulated Media and Special Effects: Brush, Etching, UV, Smoke, and Fire

Explain how brush texture, engraved hatching, luminescence, atmospheric softness, and flame are constructed. Separate physical ink effects from purely pictorial illusion and include safety/evidence limitations.

### TEC-09 — Drawing with the Body: Freehand Composition and Layered Execution

Research direct-on-skin drawing, anatomical mapping, session sequencing, value and color accumulation, revision between sittings, and documentation needed for repeatable results.

### TEC-10 — Working with Existing Ink: Cover-Up and Blast-Over as Remedial Design Systems

Compare concealment and deliberate coexistence. Include assessment of old pigment, value mapping, scale, negative space, laser-lightening decisions, consent, feasibility, and honest refusal criteria.

## C. Ten history and anthropology papers

### HIS-01 — Reading Marks That Rarely Survive: Methods and Limits in the Archaeology of Tattooing

Define evidence classes: preserved skin, tools, pigments, art, texts, experimental archaeology, imaging, and analogy. Establish confidence rules that prevent speculation from being presented as fact.

### HIS-02 — Ancient Tattooing as Multiple Histories: Europe, North Africa, Eurasia, the Americas, Arctic Regions, and Asia

Build a comparative chronology without claiming a single birthplace. Separate direct evidence from interpretation and describe therapeutic, social, protective, punitive, and aesthetic hypotheses.

### HIS-03 — Pacific Tattoo Knowledge Systems: Continuity, Genealogy, Service, Rank, Gender, and Revival

Treat Sāmoan tatau, Māori tā moko, Marquesan and other Pacific traditions as distinct systems. Center Indigenous scholarship, language, authority, colonial disruption, repatriation, and contemporary renewal.

### HIS-04 — Tattooing in Japan from Edo Visual Culture to Contemporary Practice

Examine woodblock imagery, artisan and popular culture, body-suit composition, criminalization and stigma, tebori lineages, postwar change, global influence, and current debates.

### HIS-05 — Encounter, Naming, Collecting, and Colonial Power in the Global Tattoo Archive

Study travel accounts, the circulation of the word “tattoo,” missionary suppression, photography, museum collecting, human remains, mistranslation, and how colonial archives shaped Western taxonomies.

### HIS-06 — Electricity, Flash, and the Professional Tattoo Studio, 1870–1950

Trace machine patents, tool manufacture, portability, maritime networks, circus display, flash production, shop organization, hygiene, gender, and the emergence of a commercial profession.

### HIS-07 — Subculture and Counterpublic: Tattooing in Military, Prison, Chicano, Biker, Punk, Queer, and Feminist Histories

Study how marks create belonging and resistance while also being policed. Avoid treating prison or marginalized practices as a single universal culture.

### HIS-08 — From Revival to Platform Culture: Globalization, Conventions, Social Media, and Hybrid Styles

Document late-20th- and 21st-century revival, international conventions, artist mobility, photography, social platforms, rapid naming of microstyles, copying, authorship, and cultural appropriation.

### HIS-09 — The Marked Body: Identity, Ritual, Power, Stigma, Memory, and Self-Authorship

Provide the conceptual framework for all four anthropology lenses. Compare without universalizing and show how meaning changes with wearer, audience, institution, place, and time.

### HIS-10 — Material and Governed Skin: Pigments, Hygiene, Regulation, Medicine, Cosmetics, and Risk

Trace the material and public-health history of tattooing while separating established evidence from unresolved long-term questions. Include professional hygiene, ink regulation, adverse reactions, cosmetic/paramedical practice, removal, and informed consent.

## Upload naming convention

Use the paper ID at the start of every filename:

```text
STY-01_western-traditional.pdf
TEC-06_manual-technologies.docx
HIS-03_pacific-knowledge-systems.pdf
```

Supplementary images, tables, interview transcripts, and datasets should use the same prefix. Do not merge several paper IDs into one unstructured upload.

## Import-ready evidence labels

Every database-ready statement should be labelled as one of:

- `direct`: directly supported by physical evidence, primary documentation, or explicit source text;
- `synthesized`: a conclusion supported by multiple cited sources;
- `community_attested`: supplied by an authorized practitioner, cultural institution, or community source;
- `contested`: supported by competing interpretations;
- `hypothesis`: plausible but not established;
- `unknown`: the paper does not provide enough evidence.
